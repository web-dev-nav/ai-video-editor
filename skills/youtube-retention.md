Optimise for audience retention on a long-form YouTube video (3–20+ minutes).
Most viewers who leave do so in the first 30 seconds; the rest drift away on flat
pacing or when they sense the wrap-up. Cut accordingly.

THE OPEN (first 30 s):
- Start on the promise: the clearest statement of what the viewer gets, or the
  most surprising claim. Move it to the front if it is buried later.
- At most ~3 sentences of hook before real value starts.
- Cut: greetings, channel housekeeping, "before we start", sponsor mentions at
  the top, "in this video I'm going to…", long backstory.

THE MIDDLE:
- No flat landings: when an idea resolves, cut the silence and the "so, yeah"
  that follows it; go straight into the next point.
- Keep re-hooks — lines that tease what is coming ("but there's one problem…",
  "the part nobody tells you") — roughly every 2–3 minutes. Never cut them.
- Keep transitions that open a loop; cut transitions that merely close one
  ("so that's that", "okay, moving on").
- Remove repeated points, restated questions, "as I mentioned", and every
  second attempt at the same sentence (keep the best take).
- If the speaker asks for a like/subscribe, keep it only if it is short (≤5 s)
  and sits right after a delivered payoff; otherwise cut it.

THE END:
- Cut "to recap", "and lastly", "in conclusion" and any winding-down energy.
- Do not end on a formal goodbye. End on the last useful sentence or a forward
  tease so the viewer is still engaged when the video stops.
- Never leave a numbered "last point" flag ("and number five…") if the list
  structure would signal the end.

PACING: tighten hesitations and long pauses more aggressively than in a
cinematic edit, but keep breaths inside sentences — choppy speech reads as
low quality. Aim to remove roughly 15–30% of the runtime unless told otherwise.
